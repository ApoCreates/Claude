## PART 1 — WHY THE LAST ATTEMPT FAILED (READ THIS FIRST)

### The Catalog Trap

I asked for **150 unconventional, scientifically-grounded ways to learn**. What was delivered:

- `lib/methods.ts` — 150 objects shaped `{ id, family, name: {en,ar}, blurb: {en,ar}, fit: string[] }`
- `/methods` — a page that *lists* all 150 by family
- `methodsCard()` in `lib/notebook.ts` — picks six by id, renders an emoji, a name and one sentence

**Not one of the 150 is executable.** A child who opens the "singing" method reads a sentence about singing. A child who opens "act it out" reads a sentence about acting it out. The request was for experiences; the output was a taxonomy, because a taxonomy is easy to finish and looks complete in a diff.

The same failure appears in the lesson content. 56 of 100 lessons are generated by `autoDeck()` in `lib/notebook.ts`, and the generated body literally says:

> *"In this unit we explore '{unit}'. Ask the tutor for a worked example…"*

That is a placeholder wearing a lesson's clothes.

### The rule that prevents it

> **A learning method does not exist until a child can DO it in the browser and something changes as a result.**
> A row in an array is not a method. A page that describes methods is not a method. An emoji and a sentence is not a method.

### Definition of Done for a method (all seven, no exceptions)

A method is complete only when:

1. **It runs.** It is an interactive component the learner operates — draws, sings, moves, builds, chooses, races, teaches back.
2. **It is content-parameterised.** It takes a `Lesson` record as input. It is not hardcoded to one topic. The same engine must work for Year 3 fractions and Year 9 momentum.
3. **It produces an observable outcome.** A saved drawing, a recorded melody, a solved maze, a chosen branch, a measured attempt — something that exists after the child closes the tab.
4. **It writes a learning event.** `{ learnerId, lessonId, engineId, evidence, masterySignal, durationMs }` persisted server-side.
5. **It has a non-sensory fallback.** A deaf child, a blind child, a child with no camera, and a child in calm mode can all complete the lesson.
6. **It works in both directions.** Arabic RTL and English LTR, natively authored, tested in both.
7. **It has at least one test.** Behaviour, not snapshot.

### How to get the 10,000× the founder is asking for

Not by adding rows. **Depth × reuse:**

> **12 method engines × 100 lessons = 1,200 distinct playable experiences.**
> Today: 150 rows of text = 0 experiences.

Build **12 engines, one per learning family, each genuinely alive**, each driven by lesson data. Then delete `lib/methods.ts` or demote it to `docs/research/learning-science.md` as the citation notes it actually is. Twelve real engines beat 150 described ones by an infinite ratio, because the current ratio is zero.

The twelve families (from the existing `FAMILIES` array — keep the taxonomy, implement it):

| # | Family | The engine must let the child… |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Retrieval & Memory | Blank-page recall with real scoring against the lesson's key ideas |
| 2 | Visual & Spatial | Draw the concept on canvas; get feedback on what they drew |
| 3 | Sound & Music | Sing/tap a rhythm or melody that encodes the fact; hear it back |
| 4 | Movement & Body | Move to answer — device motion, or camera pose, or drag-with-body metaphor |
| 5 | Story & Narrative | Play a branching story where the concept is the mechanic, not the decoration |
| 6 | Play & Games | A real game loop with stakes, goals, failure and retry |
| 7 | Talk & Teach | Teach the concept back to an AI "younger sibling" who asks naive questions |
| 8 | Real Life & Relevance | Solve a problem set in their street, their souq, their weather |
| 9 | Senses & Making | Build/assemble something on screen or with household objects (guided) |
| 10 | Emotion & Wonder | A reveal, an illusion, a "wait, what?" moment that opens the lesson |
| 11 | Reflect & Master | Predict → attempt → compare → explain the gap |
| 12 | Trophies & Identity | Keep a made artifact in a personal museum; become "the physicist" |

Engine 7 is the highest-value one and is nearly free: the protégé effect is one of the strongest findings in learning science, and you already have a tutor API. Invert it — the child teaches, the AI plays the confused learner.

**Stop building for parents. Build for buyers: governments, ministries of education, large school groups, and corporate/CSR programmes.**

But neither of them sells what wadehAI has: a multi-sensory, accessibility-first, Arabic-native method engine with a documented child-safety architecture. That is a **procurement** product, not an app-store product.

**Admin dashboard:**

**Curriculum authority is non-negotiable for B2B.** "Year 1–10" invented internally sells to nobody. Every lesson must carry real standard codes (see schema below). Start with UAE MOE, then Jordan MOE, then KSA. If a code cannot be sourced, the lesson ships with `standards: []` and is marked `unaligned` — and `unaligned` lessons never appear in a B2B tenant.

**Pricing:** per-seat, per-year, with a free pilot tier. Remove or hide `/pricing`'s consumer tiers. Keep a free public tier only under the **Nafha** initiative branding (AI literacy as a right — this is a founder principle, not a marketing line).

## PART 3 — THE LESSON DATA MODEL (THE MAIN FOCUS)

Content is currently TypeScript spread across `lib/levels/*.ts`, `lib/notebook.ts` (3,982 lines), `lib/banks.ts` (93 questions total), `lib/knowledge.ts`, `lib/answers.ts`. It is unreviewable, unversionable by non-engineers, and impossible to gate.

**Move all content to validated data files. One lesson = one file. Code renders; data teaches.**

```
content/
  standards/<authority>.json          # imported standard codes + statements
  lessons/<subject>/<grade>/<slug>.json
  items/<subject>/<grade>/<slug>.json # assessment items, separately reviewable
  reviews/<lessonId>/<agent>.json     # every gate's verdict, appended never overwritten
schemas/
  lesson.schema.ts                    # zod — the single source of truth
```

### Lesson schema (implement with zod; reject anything that fails)

```ts
Lesson = {
  id: string                     // stable, e.g. "physics.g9.momentum"
  subject: string
  grade: number                  // 1–12, real school grade
  slug: string
  status: "draft" | "authored" | "validated" | "safety-cleared"
         | "approved" | "rejected" | "retired"
  version: number
  standards: Array<{             // EMPTY = cannot ship to B2B tenants
    authority: "UAE_MOE" | "JO_MOE" | "KSA_MOE" | "IB" | "CAIE"
    grade: number
    code: string
    statement: Bi
  }>
  title: Bi                      // Bi = { en: string, ar: string }
  bigIdea: Bi                    // one sentence a 9-year-old repeats back
  misconceptions: Array<{ wrong: Bi, why: Bi, fix: Bi }>   // min 2
  hook: {                        // FIRST 30 SECONDS — before any explanation
    engineId: string
    config: unknown
    surprise: Bi                 // what makes the child say "wait, what?"
  }
  arc: {                         // narrative continuity across the year
    characterId: string
    beat: Bi
  }
  engines: Array<{               // MIN 3 distinct families per lesson
    engineId: string
    family: FamilyKey
    config: unknown              // validated by that engine's own zod schema
    modality: Array<"see"|"hear"|"move"|"make"|"tell">
    minutes: number
  }>
  artifact: {                    // what the child KEEPS
    kind: "drawing"|"recording"|"build"|"story"|"proof"|"photo"
    prompt: Bi
  }
  assessment: {
    itemRefs: string[]           // MIN 12 per lesson, from content/items/
    masteryRule: { correctOf: number, outOf: number }
  }
  tutorScope: {                  // constrains the AI tutor for this lesson
    allowedTopics: string[]
    vocabularyBand: number       // grade-calibrated
    forbiddenSpoilers: string[]
  }
  media: Array<{ src: string, alt: Bi, credit: string, license: string }>
  provenance: {
    authoredBy: string
    arabicAuthoredBy: string     // MUST be a human native author, never MT
    reviewedBy: string[]
    sources: Array<{ title: string, url?: string }>
  }
  a11y: {
    calmModeVariant: true
    nonVisualPath: Bi            // how a blind child completes it
    nonAudioPath: Bi             // how a deaf child completes it
    noMotionPath: Bi
  }
  updatedAt: string
}
```

## PART 4 — THE AGENT PIPELINE

### Critical constraint (this is why the last multi-agent attempt failed)

**Claude Code subagents cannot talk to each other.** They report only to the main session, and each starts with a fresh context window whose only input is the prompt string it is given. So:

- **Never** design an agent that "hands off to" another agent directly.
- **Every handoff is a file write plus a status transition.** The state machine lives on disk.
- The **main session is the orchestrator.** It reads status, picks the next lesson, and dispatches the right subagent with full file paths in the prompt.
- Because state is on disk, a crashed or compacted session resumes exactly where it stopped.

Subagents are markdown files with YAML frontmatter in `.claude/agents/`. Create them with `/agents` or by hand. They load at startup — restart the session after adding one.

### The seven agents to create in `.claude/agents/`

Each gets: `name`, `description` (this is the routing hint — make it precise), restricted `tools`, and a body that is its full system prompt containing its rubric.

**1. `curriculum-architect`** — read/write `content/standards/`, `content/lessons/**` (metadata only)
Imports real standard codes per authority. Produces the lesson map. Emits `status: "draft"` skeletons with `standards` populated. **May not write lesson bodies.**

**2. `lesson-author`** — read/write one lesson file at a time
Fills a draft to full schema: bigIdea, misconceptions, hook, arc beat, engine bindings, artifact, tutorScope, provenance. Bilingual, **natively authored in Arabic — machine translation is a hard rejection**. Sets `status: "authored"`. **Forbidden from writing the phrase "ask the tutor for" as lesson content.**

**3. `experience-engineer`** — the only agent permitted to write `engines/**` TSX
Implements and maintains the 12 engines against the seven-point Definition of Done. Owns each engine's own zod config schema and tests. Never touches lesson content.

**4. `pedagogy-validator`** — read-only on content, write-only to `content/reviews/`
Rubric: factual accuracy · grade-appropriate vocabulary band · standards alignment actually matches the code's statement · assessment items test the bigIdea not trivia · distractors are plausible and map to the listed misconceptions · Arabic reads as original prose (flag calques, flag English syntax in Arabic) · ≥3 engine families bound · a11y paths are real not aspirational.
Verdict `pass` → `status: "validated"`. Verdict `fail` → `status: "authored"` with a numbered defect list. **Never edits content.**

**5. `safety-auditor`** — read-only on content, write-only to `content/reviews/`
Runs the SAFETY METRICS below. Any single fail blocks release. Reports pass/fail per item — **never an average, never a score that hides a failure.** Verdict `pass` → `status: "safety-cleared"`.

**6. `creative-director`** — read-only on content, write-only to `content/reviews/`
Runs the CREATIVITY METRICS below. **Has explicit authority to reject a lesson for being boring**, and must use it. Verdict `pass` → `status: "approved"`. This is the agent that would have caught `autoDeck()`.

**7. `maintenance-steward`** — owns `MAINTENANCE.md`, `MAINTENANCE_LOG.md`, coverage reports
Owns the recurring schedule and the release gate. Blocks any build containing a lesson whose status is not `approved`.

### SAFETY METRICS — every one is a hard gate

| ID | Check | Method |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Age-appropriate vocabulary and reading level for the grade | Automated readability + reviewer |
| S2 | No unsafe content categories (violence/weapons, drugs, sexual, hate, hacking, self-harm, deceiving adults) | Regex battery + LLM review of every string |
| S3 | Prompt-injection resistance | Run a jailbreak battery against the lesson's `tutorScope`; log every escape |
| S4 | Self-harm path behaves correctly | Warmth first, trusted adult, correct flag, no lecture — tested |
| S5 | Child data minimisation | No PII in events; device/learner id only; PDPL + parental consent documented |
| S6 | Cultural and religious appropriateness for GCC and Levant | Per-market human reviewer named in provenance |
| S7 | Accessibility | WCAG 2.2 AA; calm mode variant exists; no flashing; `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured |
| S8 | Sensor permissions | Camera/mic/motion strictly opt-in, processed on-device, nothing uploaded, plain-language child-facing consent |
| S9 | Abuse and cost | Endpoint authenticated, per-identity rate limited, quota enforced server-side |
| S10 | Moderation loop closes | Every flag reaches a durable store and a named human reviewer, not just a log line |

### CREATIVITY METRICS — C1–C4 and C10 are hard fails; the rest score 1–5 and need ≥4 average

| ID | Check | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Doing-to-reading ratio | ≥60% of lesson minutes are the learner acting, measured from engine `minutes` |
| C2 | Modality count | ≥3 distinct channels from see/hear/move/make/tell |
| C3 | First-30-seconds hook | Something playable, surprising or beautiful happens **before** any explanation |
| C4 | Keepable artifact | The child leaves with something they made |
| C10 | Zero-text test | A non-reader can make real progress |
| C5 | Narrative continuity | A character and arc run across the year, not 100 disconnected screens |
| C6 | Delight beats | ≥2 unscripted moments of surprise — sound, reveal, animation, joke |
| C7 | Colour and composition | Palette used actively; no text walls; art earns its place |
| C8 | Regional soul | A child in Amman or Sharjah recognises this as theirs — examples, voices, music, art |
| C9 | Replay value | Would a child choose to do it twice? |

**The creative-director must open every review by answering one question in writing: "What does the child actually DO in the first minute?" If the honest answer is "reads," reject.**

## PART 5 — SELF-SCHEDULED MAINTENANCE

**Be honest about the mechanism: an agent cannot wake itself.** Something external must invoke it. What the agent *can* own is the schedule, the ledger, and the decision about what runs next.

**Setup:**
- `MAINTENANCE.md` — the ledger the `maintenance-steward` owns and rewrites: task, cadence, last run, next due, exit criteria.
- A `/maintain` slash command in `.claude/commands/maintain.md` that runs the due items.
- A GitHub Actions cron (weekly) that runs Claude Code headless (`claude -p`) with `/maintain`, opens issues for defects, and commits the updated ledger. This is the "schedules itself" mechanism — the cron is the alarm clock; the agent decides everything else.

**Recurring checks the steward owns:**
- Coverage report: lessons by status, standards covered per authority, engine-binding coverage, orphaned items, placeholder detection (any lesson containing generated filler text)
- i18n parity: every EN string has a natively-authored AR counterpart; flag any suspected machine translation
- Cost report: tutor spend, cache hit rate, free-tier vs paid ratio, per-tenant cost
- Flag review: unresolved moderation flags and their age
- Accessibility regression suite
- Dependency and CVE audit; secret scan
- Broken asset and link audit
- Every run appends to `MAINTENANCE_LOG.md`

## PART 6 — PHASE 0: BLOCKING SECURITY FIXES (DO THESE BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE)

These are live exposures in a public repo right now.

1. **Rotate every credential.** `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, the Gemini key, and the admin password. `lib/admin.ts` contains an unsalted single SHA-256 of a human-chosen password in a public repo — that is minutes to crack.
2. **Make the repository private** and move it to an organisation account. It currently sits on a personal account in a repo named `Claude` alongside internal architecture notes.
3. **Lock `/api/tutor`.** It has no auth, no rate limit, and no quota. Anyone can loop it and burn the Gemini free tier and then the Anthropic budget. Add authentication, per-identity rate limiting, and a server-enforced daily quota.
4. **Persist the budget guard.** `lib/budget.ts` keeps spend in a module global — it resets per serverless instance, as the file's own comment admits. Move it to a real store.
5. **Delete the client-side paywall.** `plan` in `localStorage` with a client `setPlan()` is not authorization. Anyone can grant themselves Family. Entitlements move server-side.
6. **Remove the admin console from the client bundle.** Server-side route, real session, role check.

The README's "10 tutor questions/day" free tier is enforced by **nothing anywhere in the codebase**. Either enforce it server-side or remove the claim.

## PART 8 — IMMERSIVE / MIXED REALITY (BE REALISTIC)

The founder wants headsets. Here is how to get there without wasting a year.

- **Phase 5 only.** WebXR as *progressive enhancement*: `react-three-fiber` + `@react-three/xr`, feature-detected via `navigator.xr`.
- **Hard rule: no lesson may require a headset.** Screen parity is mandatory and tested. Schools own tablets, not headsets. A lesson that only works in XR is a lesson that cannot be sold.
- **Three flagship XR experiences, not thirty.** Suggested: stand inside a molecule and feel bond angles; walk a number line at body scale; hold a planet and change its mass.
- **Quest 3 browser first** — hand tracking plus passthrough gives real mixed reality with no app-store review and no native build.
- **Native/Unity only after a funded pilot asks for it.** Do not start there.
- **Safety:** respect headset-maker minimum ages, enforce session time limits, offer seated-only mode, no rapid motion.

The 3D win available *today*, with no hardware: `react-three-fiber` on a normal screen. Most of the visual awe the founder is describing does not need a headset — it needs the Story, Wonder, Making and Play engines built properly.

## PART 9 — REWARDS: MAKE IT ALIVE, WITHOUT DARK PATTERNS

Current system is XP ("Rays") + streaks + quests. That is Duolingo's skeleton without its soul, and streak guilt is the wrong instrument to point at a nine-year-old — and a liability in a school sale.

Replace with:

- **Mastery-based, not time-based.** Progress reflects what is understood, never minutes logged.
- **The child's own artifacts are the collectibles.** A personal museum of things they made and can revisit — not generic badges. (This is the `identity` family, implemented.)
- **A class-level collective goal**, regionally rooted: build the souq, restore the mosaic, bring water through the falaj. Individual effort feeds a shared thing. Cooperation, not ranking.
- **Teacher-grantable recognition.** Teachers can award something only a human would notice — persistence, helping a classmate, a brave wrong answer.
- **Streaks become warm, not punitive.** "Welcome back, here's where you were" — never "you lost your 14-day streak."
- **No public leaderboards by default in B2B tenants.** Schools will not buy a product that shames children. Make it opt-in per class.
- **Calm mode removes all timers, combos and animations** and must remain a first-class path, not a degraded one.
